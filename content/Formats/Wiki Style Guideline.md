## Part 1: Page Labels

Pages have labels, sometimes. You know on Wikipedia those things that appear at the top of the page that tells you some important info about the page itself? We have those, too!

When adding labels to the top of a page, they must go in this order when applicable. Important Page *must* be above Stub, which also *must* be above Developing Page.

Please do copy them exactly as they're written here for consistency's sake.

### Important Page

Self-explanatory. Used when a page is an important concept.

Only applies to pages under Concepts.

> [!success] This page is an important page.
> It's recommended you read through it to better understand our story and server.

### Stub

Used when a page has a lack of detail, but isn't necessarily early in development. It may just be unfinished or not up to comparative quality.

> [!faq] This page is a stub.
> Consider asking the author to improve on it, or add something yourself with their permission.

#### Deliberate Stub

Used when a page is a stub, but is meant to be. Note: Stub and Deliberate Stub can only be one or the other, not both.

> [!faq] This page is a stub, but deliberately so.
> There's nothing really more to add.

### Developing Page

Used when a page isn't necessarily a stub, but is still currently being worked on and is subject to change.

> [!info] This page is currently under construction.
> Changes will be made semi-frequently.

### Unverified OC

Used when an OC is unverified.

> [!warning] Currently unverified.

### Deceased OC

Used when an OC is deceased.

> [!failure] Deceased.

### Author Notes

Species pages or concepts often have certain restrictions by the author on usage. That's where these Author Notes come in.

> [!note] NOT AN AUTHOR NOTE
> This is the only one where copying and pasting is the wrong move. Please edit the body text of the below callouts to fit your terms as an author.

> [!success] Author Note
> The Success callout is used when something is freely available and for anyone to use or suggest ideas for. This is the most lenient of them all.

> [!warning] Author Note
> The Warning callout is used when the usage of the content in the page is conditional; usually asking the author or some other condition.

> [!failure] Author Note
> The Failure callout is used when something is kept to the author and feedback is either not accepted or accepted only by close people, or the content isn't usable.

### Other

Anything else takes least priority in whatever order is deemed best fit.

## Part 2: Links

The body of a page will tend to have links to other pages. To prevent clutter, here's our official stance on links:

First, a concept, species, name, or other page should only be linked once in the main body of a page. Specifically, every instance of an alias should be linked *except* for pluralization.

> [!example] Linkage Example 1.
> I really like [[Sprinklekit|Sprinklekits]].
> I love Sprinklekits so much.
> 
> Who I really don't like is [[Noite Will]].
> Noite Will did nothing and died. Not cool.

> [!example] Linkage Example 2.
> You know, [[Dire Will]] sucks. I mean, [[Dire Will|Dire]] kinda just spread [[Malice]]. We hate Malice, and we hate Dire Will.

Second, always link concepts in a callout, regardless of if they were linked first. Callouts are considered important topics, and it's more convenient to have them right there rather than have the person scroll up to the first mention to get to the page they need to.

## Part 3: Extras

We're done with the top-to-bottom stuff, now it's time for some random things I noticed.

### Callout Titles

Keep them as normal as possible. This means no formatting.

> [!success] This is fine.

> [!failure] **This** is not. It looks *weird* and `funky`.

> [!danger] PLEASE NOTE
> Do NOT use a Header as the title. Quartz incorrectly assumes it to be Tag syntax, which messes things up.

### Writing

Be verbose where you need to, be descriptive elsewhere, but do what you can to not make pages stubs. If you do, mark it as such or mark it as under development so I know not to poke at you.

Also, make sure things are understandable and clear. 

> [!failure] Bad sentence.
> If a sentence is way to long and reads like abysmal dogshii n uses way to many speling mistkes on it's way to the fnish line then i will slime you out gng

> [!success] Good sentence.
> If a sentence is too long or is obstructed by mistakes, then it isn't a good sentence. Please revise it to flow better.

> [!tip]
> Keep things to third person if you can, unless it's an author page. An anecdote from an OC is fine, a message from the author is alright, but excessive first-person use comes off as unprofessional and badly written.
