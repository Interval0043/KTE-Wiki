Most rules in KTE are highly bendable, and most laws don't really exist. However, there are a few Axioms which are undeniable truths that cannot be broken.

Each Axiom has 3 distinct sections, that of its Definition, its Application, and its Exceptions.

Maintained exclusively by [[Polytope]]. All inquiries go to him, or I. I'll use the first person quite a lot in this one.

## Axiom of Instability

>[!info] This is the thought that nothing is ever certain nor concrete in its ways, forever. Understand, though, that things may change very slowly. Or appear as if they aren't. At some point, however, things will shift, and certainties will no longer be classified as such.

While it sounds like an obvious statement, it's a rule for us. This explicitly bans immortality, immediate kills without reason, or anything that's a true absolute. However, typically we function on "reasonable change" or "implicit change" in documentation.

Implicit Change is if something is stated as being "inextinguishable" or "eternal" for example, it's always meant to have an asterisk alongside it: the axiom of instability still applies.

Reasonable Change is not setting the change manually to being absurdly slow, and this is to keep RP stable. For example: say someone creates an OC completely immune to everything except they automatically die after 7.359E78 years. Now, that's of course a contrived example, but it's useful to make my point clear. I want people to have limitations and weaknesses built in. It's fun to work around things like that, I feel.

Also, perfect processes don't exist. Nothing is fully 100% one way or another.

### Exceptions

I've said, at the beginning of this page no less, that Axioms can't be broken. But that violates the Axiom of Instability, except it doesn't. That's why we have exceptions to these rules.

Any and all Axioms, including this one, are exempt from the Axiom of Instability. This is to preserve their major goal: being the one set of things that don't change, unless we change the definition. In RP, they remain constant to the people within.

Next, any and all Axioms are immune to [[Mirror]]'s Lawbreaker Wildcard, or any other abilities that transmute / remove rules.

## Axiom of Individuality

>[!info] Each and every entity has some component of individualism. By that, everyone is unique in at least one way. No two entities can be the exact same.

This one's a lot more obvious, but when combined with the Axiom of Instability, a few things emerge. For one, perfect cloning can't exist. Two, there will never be a situation where a person will need to pick between a clone and the real one. As long as said person knows the difference, or can pick it out with recon tools, then the decision is trivial.

I really enjoy this one not for its hardline definition, but for how far I tend to take it with my [[Hedron|Hedrons]] and [[Manifold|Manifolds]]. I expect creativity from people, even if it's not a rule. See how far you can go with individuality with your OCs, or conversely, try and only use the bare definition to go in a different direction of identity struggle.

No exceptions to this one.

## Axiom of Transfer

> [!info] Any and all true destruction of matter will have it be exactly moved to Negative Space. Any and all true creation of matter will have matter pulled from Negative Space, provided said matter was first put in via destruction.

This is my answer to what we normally know as the Law of Conservation of Matter, stating that matter cannot be created nor destroyed, but simply moved from one form to another. This applies to KTE as well, through this one. There isn't much else to say in terms of application, but the Negative Space has its own Negative Entities, an entirely different classification which can be worked with.

One other good thing to note is that anything that enters Negative Space *without* being destroyed isn't at risk of being destroyed, because it isn't Negative Matter. The inverse is also true; Negative Entities can exist in regular space completely fine, just that it's a different environment and there's some challenges in that regard.
